# Track Expenses on Jobs

Source: https://rmxhelp.rentmanager.com/Topics/Express/KB/Jobs-Expenses-Track.htm

The job costing tool in Rent Manager allows you to track transactions and spending associated with specifics jobs completed by your business. For example, you can track the costs of renovations or construction projects, or examine the expenses and revenue associated with landscaping work across multiple properties. Assigning each financial transaction accordingly is the key to tracking spending on a job.

 Related Preferences

 In order to use the job costing tool in Rent Manager , Enable job costing must be checked in system preferences. For more information, refer to General Options (System Preferences) .

 Step 1: Add Transactions to the Job

 More Information

 Before you can start tracking expenses on a job, you must first create the job. For more information, refer to Add a Job .

 After your job is created, you can create various types of transactions in Rent Manager (such as bills, invoices, purchase orders, and so on) and link them to the job. The table below describes the various ways transactions can be added to a job.

 Transaction
 Description

 Bills

 When creating bills, you can use the Job column to assign line items on that bill to the job. For more information, refer to Add a One-Time Vendor/Owner Bill .

 Checks

 When writing checks, you can use the Job column to assign line items on that check to the job. For more information, refer to Write a Vendor/Owner Check .

 Credit Card Transactions

 When adding transactions to a credit card, you can use the Job column to assign line items on that transaction to the job. For more information, refer to Add a Credit Card Transaction .

 Invoices

 When creating invoices, you can use the Job field to link that invoice to the job. For more information, refer to Add an Invoice .

 Issue Work Orders

 When creating service issues, you can use the Work Order tile to add line items and opt to assign those work order line items to the job using the Job column. For more information, refer to Add a Work Order .

 Journal Entries

 When adding journal entries, you can use the Job column to assign line items on that journal to the job. For more information, refer to Add a Journal Entry .

 Purchase Orders

 When creating purchase orders (POs), you can use the Job column to assign line items on that PO to the job. For more information, refer to Add a Purchase Order .

 Step 2: Link Additional Job Information

 After your job is created, you also have the option to link it to various service issues or projects, and record vendor credits and deposits on the job. The table below describes the other information you can track add to a job.

 Option
 Description

 Other Income Deposits

 When depositing payments, if you select the Add Other Income button, you deposit income that isn't directly related to a tenant or prospect and link it to a job in the Job column. For more information, refer to Add Other Income to a Deposit .

 Workflow Projects

 If you have a workflow project that is related to a job and you want most or all of the issues added to that workflow project to be linked to the job, you can assign that job as the default for the workflow project. From the workflow project's details, click Settings and, in the Default Issue Values section, use the Job field to set the default job to assign to issues. For more information, refer to Workflow Projects and Templates .

 Service Issues

 When creating service issues, you can link the job to the issue on the Issue Details & Info tile in the Job field. For more information, refer to Add an Issue .

 Vendor Credits

 When adding a credit from a vendor, you can use the Job field to link that credit to a specific job. For more information, refer to Add a Vendor Credit .

 Step 3: Track Job Expenses with Reports

 Once you started recording expenses and other information on a job, you can use Rent Manager reporting to track information about the job and its related transactions. The relevant job-specific reports are described in the table below.

 Report
 Description

 Job Balance Sheet

 Provides a breakdown of the assets of one or more jobs and the claims (liabilities and equities) against those assets. For more information, refer to Job Balance Sheet (Report) .

 Alternatively, you can use the Job Balance Sheet Comparison report to compare this information between multiple jobs (up to 12). For more information, refer to Job Balance Sheet Comparison (Report) .

 Job Budget Comparison

 Compares the amounts you budgeted for each general ledger (GL) account for one or more jobs against to the actual amount that has been spent on the job(s). For more information, refer to Job Budget Comparison (Report) .

 Job Cash Flow

 Examines how the cash balance of a job is impacted by changes, providing a clear picture of how the company is receiving and spending cash on a job. For more information, refer to Job Cash Flow (Report) .

 Job Cost by Vendor

 Provides a breakdown of each vendor that contributed to the expenses of a single job, allowing you to calculate how much each vendor was paid to do their portion of the job. For more information, refer to Job Cost by Vendor (Report) .

 Job General Ledger

 Examines one or more general ledger (GL) accounts that have transactions assigned to a job so you can see the financial impact of the job on each account. For more information, refer to Job General Ledger (Report) .

 Job Profit & Loss

 Examines the income and expenses of a specific job over a date range. For more information, refer to Job Profit & Loss (Report) .

 Alternatively, you can use the Job Profit & Loss Comparison report to compare and contrast income and expense information between two similar jobs, or you can use Job Profit & Loss Summary report to view a high overview of the income and expenses of multiple jobs. For more information, refer to Job Profit & Loss Comparison and Job Profit & Loss Summary (Report) .
